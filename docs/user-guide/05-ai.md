# The AI features

Frontier can use an AI to help you work with your world: answering questions from your own entries,
suggesting dates and types, helping you rewrite a passage, drafting a new entry from an idea,
speaking as one of your characters, and proposing places to discover on a map. This chapter tells
you how to set it up, what each feature does, what it costs, and what is sent where.

**The rules that never change**

- **The AI never writes into your library on its own.** Everything it makes becomes a *proposal* or an
  *unsaved change* that you read, as a difference against what you have, and then keep or discard.
  Nothing is part of your library until you save a version.
- **It reads your published entries**, not your unsent drafts, and never the appendices.
- **It is marked when it guesses.** An answer paragraph that no passage of your entries supports is
  labelled "A guess".
- **You set a monthly spending cap.** When it is reached, AI stops until the month turns or you raise
  the cap.

## Set up in five minutes

1. **Get a key** from an AI provider. The easiest is OpenRouter (see [How to get a
   key](#how-to-get-a-key)).
2. Open **Settings, Providers**, choose the provider, paste the key, and press **Check and save**.
3. Open **Settings, Models**. For each task, press **Use ..., recommended**, or choose a model
   yourself.
4. Open **Settings, Spending** and check the monthly cap. The starting cap is $10.
5. Press **Try it** beside a model to make one small test call.

You are done. The AI features now appear: the **Ask** place in the left bar works, entries about
people and deities offer **Talk with ...**, and the editor's **Help** works.

**Tip:** If you are not the person who runs the server and Settings says "A key cannot be stored
here: the server has no SECRETS_KEY", ask the administrator to set one (see the Administration
chapter), or to put a key in the server's environment.

## Providers and keys

A **provider** is the company whose AI service Frontier calls on your behalf. You use **your own
key**, so the usage is billed to your account with that provider. Frontier does not provide AI of its
own.

Open **Settings, Providers**. Each provider row shows:

- **Connected** (green), **No key**, **Switched off**, **Key refused**, or "The stored key can't be
  read. Enter it again.";
- the end of its key (only the last few characters are ever shown) and when it was last checked;
- whether the provider **reports what each call costs**, or Frontier **estimates the cost from
  prices**;
- which tasks use it.

### Which providers

| Provider | What it is | Cost is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **OpenRouter** | One key, hundreds of models from every maker. The easiest start | Reported by the provider |
| **Anthropic** | Claude models, directly | Estimated from prices |
| **OpenAI** | GPT and image models, directly | Estimated |
| **Google AI Studio** | Gemini models, directly | Estimated |
| **xAI** | Grok models, directly | Estimated |
| **Mistral** | Mistral's models, directly | Estimated |
| **DeepSeek** | DeepSeek's models, directly | Estimated |
| **Groq** | Fast open models | Estimated |
| **Other, OpenAI-compatible** | Any service that speaks OpenAI's chat format, such as one you run yourself. You give its address and, if it needs one, a key. You can add several | Estimated |

You can connect more than one provider and use different ones for different tasks.

### How to get a key

You make a key on the provider's website, then paste it into Frontier. The usual steps are the same
everywhere: create an account, add a payment method or some credit, open the page for API keys,
create a key, and copy it (it is shown once).

| Provider | Where keys are made | What a key usually starts with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OpenRouter | openrouter.ai/keys | `sk-or-` |
| Anthropic | console.anthropic.com | `sk-ant-` |
| OpenAI | platform.openai.com | `sk-` |
| Google AI Studio | aistudio.google.com | `AIza` |
| xAI | console.x.ai | `xai-` |
| Mistral | console.mistral.ai | (varies) |
| DeepSeek | platform.deepseek.com | `sk-` |
| Groq | console.groq.com | `gsk_` |

**Recommended first provider: OpenRouter.** One key gives you models from many makers, so you can try
different ones for different tasks without more accounts, and it reports the exact cost of each call.

A key is **a password for your account's spending**. Do not share it or paste it anywhere but
Frontier. Frontier will warn you if what you paste does not look like a key from that provider ("...
keys usually start with ..."); the warning does not stop you.

### Add a provider

1. In **Settings, Providers**, under **Connect a provider** (or **Add another provider**), press the
   provider.
2. Paste the key. Use the eye button to show or hide it.
3. Press **Check and save**. The server asks the provider for its list of models, which costs nothing,
   and only saves the key if the provider accepts it.

If the provider cannot be reached to check the key, you are offered **Save anyway**. If it refuses
the key, the provider's own message is shown (with anything that looks like a key removed) and
nothing is saved.

For **Other, OpenAI-compatible**, you also give the **Address** (it must start with `https://`) and
a **Name**, such as "Home server". Addresses on private or local networks are refused for safety. A
key is optional for services that do not need one.

### Change, check or remove a key

On each provider row:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Check again** | Asks the provider whether the key still works |
| **Replace key** / **Add key** | Opens a box to give a new key |
| **Switch off** / **Switch on** | Stops using it without losing the key |
| **Remove** | Deletes the key from the server. If tasks use it, you are told they will turn off |

A key given in the server's environment shows "from the server's environment" and is changed by the
administrator, not here. A key you store on this page takes precedence over one in the environment.

**Where keys live.** Keys you enter here are encrypted on the server and never sent back to your
browser; only their last characters are shown.

## Choosing models

A **model** is the particular AI inside a provider. Frontier uses one model for each of three tasks.
Choose them in **Settings, Models**.

| Task | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Proposing fields** | Reads an entry and suggests dates and types for the inbox |
| **Lore conversations** | Ask, writing help, talking to characters, exploring a map, and comments |
| **Concept art** | Pictures for discoveries, made when you ask |

For each task you see the chosen model, its context size, and its price (for example "$3 in, $15 out"
per million tokens; "≈" before a price means the cost is estimated). The buttons beside it:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Change** | Opens the model picker |
| **Try it** | Makes one small real call to prove it works, and shows the answer and what it cost. For concept art it asks first, "A picture costs a few cents." |
| **Turn off** | Switches the task off |

A choice saves as you make it ("Saved"), and you get an **Undo**. If a task is off, you see **Use ...,
recommended** (Frontier's pick among your connected providers) and **Choose a model**.

### The model picker

- **Search models** finds by name.
- **Sort:** **Recommended**, **Price** or **Name**.
- **Fits this task** (on by default) hides models that cannot do the task: for lore and fields they must
  be able to give fixed-form answers; for art they must make pictures. Press **Show them** to see
  the rest.
- **Reads images** (not for art) keeps models that can read pictures.
- One chip for each connected provider narrows by provider.
- Models marked **Recommended** are Frontier's pick for that task, reviewed in November 2026.
- The details button on a model shows its price, context size, what it reads and answers, whether it
  thinks before answering, whether the provider reports its cost, and an estimate: "A typical lore
  question here sends about N tokens ... about $0.0X each. Your cap of $10 covers about N."
- If you know a model's name that is not listed, type it and press **Try "..." as a model name**. It is
  chosen only if it answers.
- If a model's price is not known, Frontier asks you to type it (dollars per million tokens, **In** and
  **Out**) before you can choose it, so its cost can count against your cap. 0 is fine for a free
  model.

**Which model should I pick?** A small, fast, cheap model is fine for **Proposing fields**. For **Lore
conversations**, pick a stronger one: it has to read many entries carefully and stay faithful to
them. Frontier's recommendations follow this. Start with the recommended ones.

If a model disappears from its provider, the page warns: "Not listed by ... any more; calls may fail.
Choose another model."

## Cost, the cap and the log

AI calls are paid per use, to the provider, with your key. Frontier shows what it is spending and
stops at a limit you set.

### The monthly cap

In **Settings, Spending**, a line reads "**$1.23** of a **$10** cap · November 2026". The cap is a box:

- Type a new amount in US dollars and press Enter or click away. It saves.
- A meter shows how much of the cap is used. It resets on the 1st of next month (months are counted in
  UTC).
- If you set the cap lower than what you have already spent this month, you are warned that AI stops
  until next month.

**What happens at the cap.** Every AI call is checked first. If the month's spending is at or over the
cap, the call is refused with a message such as "This month's AI spending is $10.02 and your cap is
$10.00. Raise the cap on the settings page to go on." Nothing is sent to the provider.

**Important:** The cap is Frontier's own. Also set a hard limit with each provider (the Spending page
links to each provider's limits page). Costs that Frontier cannot know (a model with no known price)
count as zero toward the cap.

### How the cost is known

| Shown as | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `$0.04` | The provider told Frontier exactly (OpenRouter does) |
| `≈ $0.04` | Estimated from the model's price and the tokens used |
| unknown | No price is known: not counted toward the cap |
| failed: ... | The call failed (it did not cost anything counted) |

### By provider and the log of calls

- **By provider** lists, for the chosen month, each provider's calls and cost, and how the cost is
  known ("reported by the provider", "estimated from prices (≈)", "n with no price known: not
  counted").
- **Calls** is the **AI log**: one row per call, with **When**, **What**, **Model**, **Tokens** (sent / received) and **Cost**.
  "What" says in words: "Lore conversation" with the entry's name, "Proposed fields", "Concept art",
  "Exploring a map", "Talking with ...", or "Try it".
- Use the arrows (**‹ October** and **December ›**) to look at other months. Tick **Failed only** to find
  problems. Hover a failed row's cost to see its error.

The log keeps the call's details (time, model, tokens, cost, which entry) and error messages. It does
not keep what you asked or the answers; lore conversations are kept separately (see [Ask the
lore](#ask-the-lore)).

## Ask the lore

**Ask** (left bar) answers questions about your world **from your own published entries**, and shows
its sources.

### Ask a question

1. Open **Ask**. The page is titled **Ask the lore**.
2. Type in **A question or an idea**, for example "How do the Nirani keep time?"
3. Press **Ask** (or Ctrl+Enter).

If no model is chosen for lore conversations, a banner says so with a link, **Choose one in
Settings**, and the buttons stay off.

### While it works

Under your question you see the **steps** as they happen: names found in your question, entries
searched, entries read, the model asked, and quotes checked. If the model thinks aloud, a **Thinking**
section shows it. A line shows "Working · 12s", then "Writing the answer · 1,200 characters". Press
**Stop** to cancel; nothing is kept.

### Reading an answer

An answer is made of paragraphs. Each shows:

- the paragraph, as plain text;
- **quotes** from your entries, with the entry each is from (checked to appear word for word);
- **From** and the entries it rests on.

A paragraph is "from the lore" only if one of its quotes (four words or more) really is in one of its
own sources. Otherwise it is marked **"A guess: no passage of the entries was found to support
this."** and looks different. Treat guesses as the AI's suggestion, never as your lore.

Under the answer:

- **This is not in the lore.** appears when nothing supports the answer, or when the AI says what is
  missing ("The AI says it lacks: ..."). A button **Draft an addition** is offered.
- **Also related:** lists other entries that bear on the question, with a few words on why.
- A line **Read N entries · thought for 12s · $0.012** opens to show the steps again and which
  entries were read. If a quote was not found, it says "1 quoted passage was not found in the entries
  and left out."

An entry that has since been removed appears struck through.

### Follow up

Press the title of a conversation to continue it. **Ask again** takes a follow-up; the AI remembers
the last answers' supported content (never its guesses). **Delete the conversation** removes the
questions and answers (additions you kept stay among your drafts).

**Conversations** (the list under the box on **Ask**) shows each with its number of questions and its
date. They are working notes, not lore: kept on the server and included in backups, but not in your
library or its history.

### Draft an addition

When an answer shows what is missing, **Draft an addition** asks the AI to write text that would fill
the gap, in the style of your library.

1. Under **Drafted addition**, choose **Where it goes**: an entry the answer was read from, or the new
   entry the AI proposed.
2. Read the **difference** (your entry now, and with the addition at the end).
3. Press **Keep as a draft** to make it an unsaved change, or **Draft again** for a different attempt.

A banner then says "Kept as a draft of ... Read it among the unsaved changes before you save a
version." (The left bar calls this **Unsaved**, or **Unpublished** if you use GitHub.) Nothing is in
your library until you save a version.

If the entry changed since the AI wrote the addition, Keep is refused with a message and the
difference is shown again.

### From the editor

In the editor, select a passage and choose **Ask the lore about this...** from its menu. Ask opens with
the passage quoted, ready for your question.

## Draft an entry from an idea

On **Ask**, the box takes ideas as well as questions. Type or speak an idea ("A river spirit the
Nirani only meet at night, who...") and press **Draft entry**.

Frontier looks up related entries, then proposes a **new entry**: a title, a folder, and short
paragraphs that stay close to what you said. You see:

- **Looked up the lore:** the entries it consulted.
- **Folder** and **Title** (you can change both). If an entry with that title exists, you are told and
  keeping is refused.
- Each **paragraph**, editable in place.
- **Not said yet:** things an entry like this would need that you did not say. The AI does not invent
  them.
- **Links:** names it links to, marked "would be new" if no such entry exists.

### Edit and recheck

- Change any paragraph by typing. When you leave it, that paragraph alone is **rechecked against the
  lore** ("Checking against the lore..."), and marked "Edited by you. Rechecked." **Undo** puts it back.
- **Change this...** under a paragraph lets you say or type a change ("make her the eldest warden"),
  or pick **Shorter**, **Simpler** or **More vivid**. The **Suggested change** is shown word by word.
  **Accept** it, **Edit first**, or **Dismiss**.

### Conflicts with the lore

If a paragraph contradicts an existing entry, it is marked **Conflicts with the lore**, with the entry's
own words quoted and a link to it. You must decide each one before you can keep the draft:

- **Fix it for me** asks for a minimal change so it agrees with the entry.
- **Keep mine anyway** keeps your line. The file records a hidden note ("kept against the lore") so the
  decision is visible later.

### Keep it

Press **Keep as unsaved change**. The new entry waits among your unsaved changes; nothing enters the
library until you save a version. **Discard** throws the draft away. After keeping, **Ask another**
starts afresh.

Each recheck and each change suggestion is a small AI call and counts against the cap.

## Talk with a character

For an entry whose type is **person** or **deity**, the entry page shows **Talk with *Name***
(when a lore model is chosen). You speak with the character, in their voice, from a moment in time
you choose.

### First time: the profile

The first visit asks you to make the character's **profile**, which is what they know and how they
speak.

1. Press **Draft a profile from the entries**. Frontier reads the character's entry and related ones
   and proposes **traits**. Each trait is one of:
   - **How they speak**
   - **What they know**
   - **What they believe**
   - **Not known** (things the entries leave open)
2. Each trait shows **From** and the entries (and a quote) it came from. Edit the text, change the
   kind, **Remove** it, or **Add a trait**.
3. **Speaks from:** describe the moment, for example "the year before the Exodus".
4. Optionally give that moment's year in the main chronicle. Entries whose event in the main chronicle
   begins after that year are kept from the character, so they do not know the future.
5. Press **Approve the profile**. It is saved in the library with your next version.

Press **Change the profile** later to edit it.

### The conversation

Type under **Say something to *Name*** and press **Say it**. The character answers in their voice.

- A line drawn from the entries shows **From** the entries.
- A line the AI made up is marked **Improvised: nothing in the entries says this.**
- **Add to *Name*'s entry** puts a line into the character's entry as an unsaved change. It then says
  "Added to ...'s entry as an unsaved change."

**This conversation is not kept:** it ends when you leave the page. Lines you added stay among your
unsaved changes.

## Writing help in the editor

When you edit an entry, **Help** (the sparkle in the toolbar, or **Help with this...** in the editor
menu) helps you rewrite a passage. Its shortcut is **Ctrl+J** (**Cmd+J** on a Mac).

### Use it

1. Select a passage, or put the cursor in a paragraph.
2. Press **Help**. A card opens right under the passage, which is highlighted.
3. Say what to change in **What should change?**, for example "tell it as a legend an elder would...",
   and press **Go**; or press a ready-made request: **Shorter**, **Longer**, **Simpler**, **More
   vivid**, **Fix spelling and grammar**.
4. Read the suggestion. **Changes** shows it word by word against your text; **Clean** shows it
   plainly.
5. Choose:
   - **Keep** replaces the passage. For six seconds a notice offers **Undo**.
   - **Try again** asks for a different suggestion. Use the arrows (‹ ›) to compare "1 of 3".
   - **Discard** closes the card.
   - **More choices, Put it below the passage** adds it after instead of replacing.
   - **Change it further...** is a box to refine the suggestion ("keep the first sentence, less formal").

The card says "Keeps every name and fact. Adds nothing new." That is the instruction given to the AI
for rewrites, though you should still read what it gives you.

### Good to know

- The longest passage is about a page (6,000 characters).
- If you change the passage after asking, the card says so and offers **Ask again with the new
  wording**.
- **Stop** (or Esc) cancels a request.
- With no model chosen, the card says "Writing help needs an AI model. Choose one once in Settings"
  with an **Open Settings** button. Offline, it says it needs a connection; your text is safe.
- The AI is given your passage, the text just before and after it, and up to six related entries so
  it keeps to the lore. Appendices are never included.

## Comments for the AI

In the editor you can leave **comments** on passages. Each is either **For me** or **For the AI**. A
comment for the AI can be turned into a suggestion: press **Suggest a change** on it and the writing
help card opens with the comment as the request. Keeping the suggestion marks the comment done.

"For the AI" is available once a lore model is chosen.

## Proposed fields: dates and types

Frontier's AI can read an entry and propose structured fields, namely **dates** ("what happened" and
"when") with the sentence they came from, and the entry's **type** (person, place, and so on). You
approve each in the **Inbox**.

### One entry

On a published entry, press **Propose fields** (shown only when AI is set up). It says "Reading the
entry...", then "Nothing new to propose." or "3 proposed. Decide in the inbox."

### Automatically on every save

In **Settings, Models**, under **Proposing fields**, tick **Propose fields for the entries of each
publish** (the wording says "publish" even for an own library's "save a version"). After each save,
the entries in that version are read in the background.

### A whole folder

**Settings, Propose for a folder** reads every published entry in a folder, one at a time.

1. Choose the **Folder** (or **The whole library**).
2. Press **Estimate**: "12 entries. Estimated cost: about $0.18. Entries read before cost nothing
   again."
3. Press **Start**. A progress line shows "Running: 3 of 12 entries read ...". **Cancel** stops it.
   It also stops by itself if the cap is reached or the AI is turned off.
4. When it is done, **Open the inbox**.

Failures are listed with the entries and reasons. A model must be chosen for **Proposing fields**
first.

## Discovery on a map

On a flat map, **Explore** (in the map's toolbar) helps you find places worth inventing: crossroads,
fords, river mouths, passes, and so on.

- Frontier works out the **points of interest** from your own roads, rivers and heights. This part is
  **free and sends nothing**.
- You **choose** one place, several, or any spot on the map. A tray lists "N places chosen".
- **What Frontier sends** opens to show exactly what would be sent for each place.
- The ask button ("Ask the AI what is there" or "Ask the AI about N places") makes one AI call, with
  your nearby entries, to propose ideas.

The ideas come back as **discovery cards**:

- "Proposed place · not in the lore", the name, and the point of interest it came from.
- A description, and **Grown from:** quotes from your entries that inspired it, with links.
- Any conflicts with the lore, in red.
- **Make concept art** (or **Draw it again**) makes a picture. This needs a model for **Concept art**
  and costs a few cents.
- **Keep it as a new entry in** (choose the folder) and **Keep** make it a new unsaved entry.
  **Discard** forgets it.
- With several waiting, "1 of 4 found" with arrows steps between them.

## Speaking instead of typing

Where you see the microphone button (the question box on **Ask**, and **Change this...** in a draft),
you can **dictate**.

- Press the microphone to start, press it again (now a stop square) to stop. The words appear in the box
  and you can edit them.
- Shortcut: **Ctrl+Shift+M** (**Cmd+Shift+M** on a Mac). **Esc** stops and keeps the text.
- On a phone, a bar with **Stop** stays at the bottom while it listens.
- Dictation uses **your browser's own** speech recognition. Some browsers send the sound to their
  maker's servers; that is the browser's choice, and Frontier does not receive audio.
- If your browser has none, the microphone is not shown and the page says "Dictation is not available in
  this browser. You can type."

## The Inbox

The **Inbox** (left bar, with a count) is where proposals wait for your decision. Accepting makes an
unsaved change; nothing enters the library until you save a version.

It can show three kinds of thing:

### Fields proposed by the AI

One section per entry: "Fields proposed by the AI from this entry's published text." Each proposal is
a **Date** (what happened and when, with the quoted sentence) or a **Type**.

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Accept** | Writes it into the entry as an unsaved change |
| **Edit** / **Done editing** | Change the wording or the value before accepting |
| **Reject** | Forgets it, and it is not proposed again |
| **Dismiss** | Shown instead when "this sentence is not in the entry any more" |

### Agent branches (GitHub libraries only)

If your library is shared through GitHub and another tool (an AI agent, for instance) proposes changes
by pushing a branch whose name begins `proposal/`, the Inbox lists it by name with its commit
messages and each file's change as a difference:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Accept** | Makes an unsaved change of that file |
| **Accept and edit** | The same, and opens the editor (notes only) |
| **Reject** | Records the decision, nothing is made |
| **Reject all that is left** / **Remove the branch** | Decides everything remaining |

Frontier takes only notes and new images from an agent. Some files can only be rejected, with the
reason shown: other kinds of file, folders the agents may not change, changes to existing appendix
files, and notes you have unsaved changes to. Deciding the last file removes the branch from GitHub.

### Arrived unreviewed (quarantine, GitHub libraries only)

When someone other than you pushes notes straight to the shared repository, they can be held as
**quarantined** (this relies on a separate sync service set up by your administrator; without it,
nothing is quarantined). The Inbox lists them under **Arrived unreviewed**, who pushed them, and the
difference.

- **Accept** keeps the note as it is.
- **Revert** undoes the push.

Until you decide, the AI features will not read a quarantined note: they say "This note arrived
unreviewed. Accept or revert it in the inbox first." A note that has just arrived is also held for a
few minutes ("This note just arrived. Try again in a few minutes."). Your decision goes out with your
next publish.

If you use Frontier's own library (the default), there are no agent branches or quarantine, only AI
field proposals.

## Privacy: what is sent where

Frontier has no AI of its own. When you use a feature, the **server** sends the request to **the
provider you chose**, with **your key**. Your browser never contacts the provider and never sees a key.

| Feature | What is sent to the provider |
| --- | --- |
| **Ask** | Your question, the last few turns of the conversation, and the published text of the entries picked for the question (about 16 at most; long entries are cut) |
| **Draft an addition / Draft entry** | What you said or the answer's gap, and the related entries' published text |
| **Talk with ...** | The profile, your message and the conversation so far, and related entries |
| **Writing help** | The passage, the text just before and after it (from your editor, including unsaved wording), your request, and up to six related published entries |
| **Propose fields** | The entry's published text |
| **Explore** | What each chosen place is (for example a crossroads of two named roads), names on the map nearby, and the nearest linked entries. **What Frontier sends** shows the text first |
| **Concept art** | A description of the discovery, with your art style text |
| **Comments for the AI** | The comment and passage, as writing help |
| **Check and save a key** | Nothing but the key: the provider's model list is requested |

Never sent: the **appendices** (the `99 Appendices` area), Frontier's own working files, and notes
that are quarantined or have just arrived. The AI reads published entries, not the unsaved drafts of
other entries.

What the provider does with what you send is governed by that provider's own terms. Many providers
offer settings to stop your data being used for training; check your account with them. If privacy is
a concern, use a provider you trust, or an endpoint you run yourself through **Other,
OpenAI-compatible**.

Frontier keeps on its own server: your encrypted keys, the AI log (without question or answer text),
your conversations, proposals and drafts. These are in the backup.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| No **Talk with** link on a person | AI must be set up (a model for Lore conversations), and the entry's type must be person or deity |
